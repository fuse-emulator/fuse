ULA sound paths
===============

ULA port writes carry two separate audio signals: D3 (MIC, active low in the
port value) and D4 (EAR/beeper).  They used to be encoded into a two-bit index
at the ULA write site and passed to sound_beeper().  That function selected a
single amplitude table and sent it to the same left/right Blip_Buffer objects
as AY and peripheral audio.  It also suppressed MIC-only output except while
playing a tape with loading sound enabled.

sound_ula() now retains the raw logical ULA states until sound_ula_update().
The tape EAR input remains a separate input; it is combined with D3 only at the
ULA output node, as in the former implementation.  Tape edge events call
sound_tape() to render that node at their exact emulated tstate.  This keeps
ROM tape load/save semantics separate from the D3 logical state.  In
particular, tape saving continues to use the ULA's raw D3 state, while an
ordinary D3-only port write is not made audible by the speaker path.  This
preserves the distinction relevant to Fuse bug #501 rather than treating
MIC-only as general speaker audio.

There are two mono ULA Blip buffers and synths:

  ula_mic_synth    -> ula_mic_buf
  ula_beeper_synth -> ula_beeper_buf

The MIC buffer represents the ULA output node and has the following steady
levels for the ULA bits (D4:D3):

  00  0
  01  SOUND_AMPL_TAPE
  10  SOUND_AMPL_BEEPER
  11  SOUND_AMPL_BEEPER + SOUND_AMPL_TAPE

Thus the electrical MIC-only state is retained.  The beeper buffer represents
the drive to the internal speaker and, without tape playback loading sound,
has these levels:

  00  0
  01  0
  10  SOUND_AMPL_BEEPER
  11  SOUND_AMPL_BEEPER + SOUND_AMPL_TAPE

MIC-only is suppressed here by explicit threshold policy, not by discarding
D3.  D3 still adds SOUND_AMPL_TAPE when D4 is high, so 10 and 11 remain
distinct. SOUND_AMPL_BEEPER is 50 * 256 and SOUND_AMPL_TAPE is 2 * 256,
retaining the 50:2 relationship.  The existing optional tape loading sound
is retained only while a tape is playing (and is disabled for Timex).  When
loading sound is disabled, the old policy of suppressing the MIC
contribution during playback is also retained.

AY, SpecDrum, Covox and SP0256 continue to render into the existing main
left/right buffers.  The selected ULA signal is rendered once per frame and
mixed identically into both final channels after the main buffers are
rendered, preserving mono ULA routing without duplicate stereo synthesis or
new inter-channel timing differences.  The rendered ULA PCM is available
through sound_ula_mic_output() and sound_ula_beeper_output(), with their
corresponding count accessors, until the next sound_frame().

The Speaker type setting selects the final ULA contribution:

  TV speaker  ula_mic_buf, unfiltered (the raw ULA/MIC socket output)
  Beeper      ula_beeper_buf, filtered as the built-in speaker
  Unfiltered  ula_beeper_buf, unfiltered internal-speaker drive

The built-in speaker model is a single second-order resonant high-pass.
Research on original 16/48K speakers is incomplete and production variation is
likely. The available evidence describes a very small moving-coil transducer
with a plastic/Mylar-like diaphragm. Issue 1/2 machines used nominally 200-ohm
units and Issue 3-6 nominally 40-ohm units, but their physical speakers appear
broadly similar. Comparable miniature Mylar speakers with roughly 10-13 mm
moving systems have a dominant mechanical resonance in the several-hundred-Hz
to approximately 1 kHz range. This supports modelling that low-frequency
transition, rather than a conventional narrow band-pass speaker.

  H(s) = s^2 / (s^2 + (w0 / Q)s + w0^2)

It uses a conventional RBJ/bilinear digital biquad at the runtime host sample
rate. The nominal experimental parameters, defined in sound/speaker_filter.h,
are f0 = 750 Hz and Q = 1/sqrt(2). The filter is applied only in the Beeper
case, immediately before final PCM mixing; it never changes ula_mic_buf. Its
state is reset on audio/AY reset and when switching between filtered and
unfiltered routes. This is a deliberately minimal acoustic model: it adds no
high-frequency low-pass, EQ peaks, enclosure response or distortion.
